React Native Development: Mobile Apps That Support Real Operations

By Abaid Ullah · Full Stack AI Software Engineer

Design around the moment the app is used

A mobile workflow may happen at a checkout counter, while scanning a physical asset or during a delivery. For a new app, I would examine that context before arranging screens. Connectivity, permissions, interruptions and the amount of information a user can enter all affect whether the workflow is practical.

Vlore is a concrete example of my React Native work: scanning, writing and managing NFC tags and physical assets. My résumé also documents NTAG 424 DNA authentication work. The business purpose is to connect a physical interaction with a trustworthy application state, which requires more than a successful scan animation.

Make failure and retry behavior part of the experience

For an app that communicates with a backend, I would define what the user sees when a request is pending or fails. A repeated tap should not leave the user uncertain about whether an action completed. My design review would cover interruption, returning to the app and refreshing data after an operation.

Fuboot adds contribution tracking and SQL optimization to my mobile experience. A similar financial tracking workflow requires clear status and understandable records. I would review edge cases with the business owner and verify access and update rules on the backend, as well as testing the mobile interaction.

Connect mobile capabilities to business friction

Tactlink is a business directory and networking app in my portfolio. Separately, my résumé documents mobile OCR work to extract business-card details and populate forms. Both are relevant to reducing manual contact entry and supporting business discovery, although they should not be assumed to be the same implementation.

My food-service work includes Keventers and Nizams applications with ordering, payment integration, tracking and updates. For a new ordering app, I would map the customer journey together with the store’s workflow. A customer-facing order state is only useful when it reflects the operation that staff can actually fulfill.

Measure completed workflows on representative devices

For a new mobile project, I would track completion rates for the central task, crash-free sessions, request failures and support issues associated with unclear state. I would test relevant device capabilities on real hardware, including NFC or camera flows where applicable, and include interrupted sessions in acceptance checks.

I would assess changes against the same workflow before and after a release, rather than using download counts alone as proof of business value. The project examples document my work and supported capabilities; they do not establish a measured increase in orders, contributions or customer satisfaction.
